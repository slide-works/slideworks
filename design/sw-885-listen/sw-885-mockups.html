SlideWorks · SW-885 · design mockups · 28 Nov 2026 · round 2 adds landscape

Live speech V2: guests listen to the talk in their own language

V1 (SW-884) puts translated subtitles on guests' phones. V2 lets a guest put headphones in and hear each translated line, read by the phone's own voice. Mobile web gets a Listen button. The SlideWorks app gets an audience mode that keeps playing with the screen locked. The speaker gets a microphone tip and a count of who is listening. The guest page, the subtitle strip and the speaker app are SW-884's mockups; only the listening parts are new.

01

What V2 ships

under the Translations epic SW-942 · after SW-884

V2 · SW-885 This Feature

  • Listen on mobile web. A button in the subtitle strip. The tap that starts it is the gesture iOS needs before it will speak.
  • The phone's own voice, picked by an explicit locale with utterance.lang set (D3). If the language has no voice, the guest is told, and Listen stays off (D13).
  • Voice and speed. Traditional Chinese readers pick Cantonese (zh-HK) or Mandarin (zh-TW).
  • Stays near live. More than 3 lines waiting skips to the latest (D9).
  • Audience mode in the app (D4): join by code or QR without signing in, follow the slides, read, and listen with the screen locked, through headphones, from the lock screen.
  • A better voice prompt where the OS has one to download.
  • Headphone counts per language in the presenter's Reading in counter (D18).
  • Landscape: the slide full screen, in the guest's language, with the translated lines over it, a listening rail and a captions menu (section 05).
  • The microphone tip the first time Live subtitles is turned on (D7, D19).

V1 · SW-884 Already there

  • The speaker app and its Subtitles sheet.
  • On-device speech to text, English and Thai.
  • Server translation per language through SW-943.
  • The subtitle strip on the guest page, and SW-883's language chip, which V2's voice follows.
  • The CC chip and Reading in counter in the presenter window.

elsewhere Not in V2

  • Cloud speech to text (Soniox): its own Feature, SW-981 (D20).
  • A local text-to-speech model for languages the phone has no voice for: SW-982.
  • Multilingual typed Q&A: V3, SW-944.
  • Server neural voices streamed as audio: later, as a premium (D3).
  • Spoken questions from guests (D8).
  • Testing microphones (D7). The tip is advice only.
  • Reading the slides aloud. Only what Sam says is spoken.

Mobile web: the Listen button

mobile web

Listen lives in the subtitle strip's header, next to Hide (D10). The first tap opens a short sheet; its Start button is the user gesture iOS needs before speechSynthesis will speak. Mobile web can't keep speaking with the screen locked, so the page holds the screen on while listening and points to the app for screen-off listening (D12).

The app's audience mode

native app · same app as the speaker's

One app, one store listing (D4). Joining needs no account, the same as mobile web; signing in is only for presenting (D14). The guest page is the web one without the address bar. While listening, a mini player sits at the bottom, and the lock screen gets Now Playing controls (D17).

Landscape: the slide full screen, the translation over it

round 2 · D22–D27 decided 28 Nov

A guest turns the phone sideways to see the slide as big as possible. SW-5 already makes the slide fill the screen, SW-883 translates it and adds a tap overlay (D6), and SW-884 puts two caption lines over it (D17). V2 has to make all of that work together while the guest listens: Sam's words, translated, over a slide that is also translated, with a way to listen, stop, resize and move the captions without turning the phone back. The status sits in the black bar beside the slide, so nothing covers the slide that doesn't have to.

Tap the phone to show the overlay, as a guest would. Tap CC in it for the captions menu.

  • The slide and the speech are both in the guest's language. The slide comes from SW-883's translations, the captions from SW-884's stream, and the voice from V2's speech queue. All three follow the one language chip.
  • Over the slide keeps the slide as big as it can be; the captions cover its bottom edge, like a film. Below the slide shrinks the slide about 18% so nothing is covered. Try it on slide 5, whose cards sit low.
  • The rail right of the slide shows listening, the language and any skipped lines (D9). It's in the black bar that a 16:9 slide leaves on a modern phone, so it never covers the slide.
  • Captions over the slide are absolutely placed, so nothing on the page moves when a line arrives. Below the slide, the band has a fixed height for each text size (D21).
  • iPhone web keeps Safari's bar: iOS has no full screen for a page. Android web has SW-883's Full screen button. The app goes full screen by itself.

States

Each is a phone turned sideways, on Thai, with Sam on slide 3 unless the caption says otherwise.

Mobile web

  • Extends GuestFollowing's landscape branch (orientation: landscape and max-height: 500px). Captions, rail and overlay are siblings of the slide, absolutely placed.
  • Caption size and position are kept in localStorage on the phone, like the Cantonese or Mandarin choice.
  • Wake Lock is already held while listening (D12). In landscape it's also held while captions are showing, because people read without touching the screen.
  • Full screen on Android is requestFullscreen() on the guest shell (SW-883 D6). It's never offered on iPhone.

The app

  • The guest screen unlocks rotation (expo-screen-orientation); join, home and speaker screens stay upright.
  • Sideways means immersive: status bar hidden, Android system bars hidden (expo-navigation-bar), home indicator auto-hidden on iOS. A swipe from the edge brings them back.
  • The screen stays awake while following a talk (expo-keep-awake). Background audio and Now Playing are unchanged (D17).
  • The mini player folds into the rail. Tapping the rail opens the overlay, not the player.

To check during planning

  • Safe areas: the island or punch hole sits left or right depending on which way the phone turned. The rail goes on the side away from it.
  • Phones with a 16:9 screen (iPhone SE) have no black bar. The rail then becomes a corner pill over the slide (D24).
  • Caption contrast on light slides: the 80% black box behind each line passes 4.5:1 on any slide.
  • iPadOS Safari does support element full screen; the Android button could show there too.
Answered 28 Nov. Q13–Q18 all went as recommended and are D22–D27 on SW-885: captions over the slide with Below as an option, the Captions menu, the rail in the black bar, captions off while listening, the app immersive when sideways, and the poll chip. See section 08.

07

How a line gets from Sam to a guest's ears

The speech queue on the guest's phone (web)

// only finals translated into my language (D11)
onCaption(c) {
  if (!listening || c.kind !== "final" || c.from === myLang) return;
  waiting.push(c);
  if (waiting.length > 3) {                 // D9
    skipped += waiting.length - 1;
    waiting = [waiting.at(-1)];
  }
  pump();
}
pump() {
  if (speechSynthesis.speaking || !waiting.length) return;
  const u = new SpeechSynthesisUtterance(waiting.shift().text);
  u.lang = voice.lang;   // "th-TH", "zh-HK": never left to the browser
  u.voice = voice; u.rate = rate; u.onend = pump;
  speechSynthesis.speak(u);
}

What changes on the wire

// guest → DeckHub, so Reading in can count headphones (D18)
SetListening({ listening: true })

// DeckHub → presenter and speaker app, with SW-884's roster
AudienceRoster({ reading: { en: 31, th: 9, ja: 5 },
  listening: { th: 4, ja: 1 } })

// nothing else: captions, groups and translation are V1's

No audio goes through SlideWorks: the voices are on the guest's phone. The server only learns who is listening, as counts.
