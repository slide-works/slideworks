What SW-2 looks like when it lands

An agent picks a template and a palette, and every slide follows them with no design input. There is no separate "theme". The template sets the structure and everything about the look except colour: fonts, shape, title art, ornament and logo. The palette sets every colour. These mockups cover the split, the built-in templates and palettes, Sanzo Wada's 348 combinations (SW-15), the chrome, deep dives, colour roles, and templates people upload, including one imported from the Northwind kickoff deck. They also cover where people choose all of this, the share card, and the agent's MCP calls. Twelve questions at the end need your call.

Two choices: a template and a palette

SW-1 ships one look (D4) with the part tracker, footer and progress bar (D5), but its colours and fonts are written into the layout CSS. SW-2 splits that look into exactly two things a deck chooses. They never overlap: a template holds no colours, and a palette holds no fonts.

Template  structure, and the look without colour

  • Layouts allowed (a subset of SW-1's 16), chrome defaults (tracker, footer, progress, draft, deep dives), an outline and house style.
  • Type: heading and body fonts, heading weight and letter spacing.
  • Shape: card radius and shadow.
  • Art: title-slide art, ornament dots, and logos for light and dark slides.
  • Optionally its own palette, which it can lock. The Northwind template does both.

Built-in: talk, workshop, update, all in SlideWorks' own type (Plus Jakarta Sans and Inter). Uploaded: any template a person makes or imports.

Palette  every colour, and nothing else

  • Surfaces and ink: paper, card, tint, grid lines, ink, muted text.
  • Accent: the accent, its ink, soft, glow and past tones, and the colour of text on it.
  • Roles: positive, negative, caution and deep dive.
  • Dark slides: night, the text on it, lines, the divider-number gradient and the stamp.
  • Panel pairs: a surface and an ink for the accent, positive, negative and inverse panels.

Built-in: Cobalt (the default, SW-1's D4 colours), Ember, Plum and Midnight. Plus Sanzo Wada's 348 combinations (SW-15, folded in), and palettes that come with an uploaded template.

SW-2 Ships in this Feature

  • Three built-in templates (talk, workshop, update) and four built-in palettes (Cobalt, Ember, Plum, Midnight).
  • Template chrome as options: part tracker, footer (n / total, title, author, logo), progress bar, draft stamp.
  • Deep-dive slides: a per-slide flag. It adds a top bar and a pill, and dividers list the upcoming deep dives.
  • Colour roles and solid tone panels (tone: accent | positive | negative | inverse), checked for contrast in every palette.
  • A headless renderer, brought forward from SW-3. preview_deck returns PNGs of the slides, and the share card comes from the same renderer.
  • MCP: list_templates, list_palettes, set_look, preview_deck, plus new fields on create_deck, add_slide and get_deck.

SW-15 Separate Feature: Sanzo Wada palettes

  • The 348 combinations as palettes, with automatic role mapping and contrast fixes (section 03). It builds on SW-2's palette model.

SW-50 New Feature: uploaded templates

  • Uploading a template, the HTML importer plus the agent route, sharing like a deck, and custom layouts as HTML with typed slots (section 07).

Elsewhere Already built, or later

  • SW-1 The 16 layouts and 5 inline blocks, the slot model (D3), and the parts list on create_deck. SW-2 adds no new layouts.
  • SW-3 Lint: checks that each slide follows the house style. The renderer moved into SW-2 (Q6).
  • SW-4 Presenter view, including "skip deep dives" while presenting.
  • SW-13 Editing in the browser. The Look panel below is the one place SW-2 adds UI controls.
  • never Free CSS or scripts inside an uploaded template. Custom layouts are allowed, as HTML with typed slots (Q9).
  • later Importing PowerPoint (.pptx / .potx), workspaces or organisations, and Wada vol. 2.
  • repo Brand templates are user content and never ship in the repo. The import example uses a made-up brand, Northwind.

Templates

Q3 asks what a template is

A template is the deck's structure plus its look without colour. It is not a set of pre-made slides. The agent gets the outline and house style back from create_deck and writes every slide itself. The three built-ins share SlideWorks' type and shape, so they differ in structure only. An uploaded template, like the Northwind one on the right, brings its own type, art, logo and palette. Every thumbnail here uses its template's default palette.

Sanzo Wada's combinations as palettes

SW-15 · separate Feature (Q12)pick any of the 348

Each combination of 2–4 colours becomes a palette. The colours are mapped to paper, ink, accent and the dark slides, and adjusted only as far as contrast needs. Positive, negative and caution keep the default role colours, because they carry meaning. A combination whose strongest colour looks like one of them isn't used as the accent if another colour will do.

Deep-dive slides

Optional material is marked rather than cut. The agent sets deep_dive: true on a slide. SlideWorks adds the coloured top bar and the "Deep dive" pill, puts the slide in the divider's "Deep dives" list, and starts the speaker notes with "Deep dive. Skip if short of time." 06

Colour roles and tone panels

Colour carries meaning, never decoration. Every palette fills five roles. SW-2 adds solid tone panels: callout, cards and the compare panels take a tone. Each tone is a surface and ink pair set by the palette, so text on a coloured panel is never unreadable.

A palette test runs the same check and fails the build, or refuses an upload, if any pair drops below its bar. The last row shows why panels need their own ink. White text on the plain positive role colour fails in Cobalt (3.3:1), Midnight (1.7:1) and Northwind harbour (3.3:1). The role colour is tuned for lines and dots, and the panel pair for text.

07

Your own template: upload one, or import it from a deck

SW-50 · new Feature (Q12)

People can upload their own template. It is data and files, never CSS or code, so SlideWorks can check it, and every layout still works with it. The easiest way to make one is to import an existing deck. Below, a company's existing HTML deck (kickoff.html) goes through the importer and becomes the template Northwind kickoff, with its own locked palette Northwind harbour. Northwind is a made-up brand. The "Ship smaller" sample deck is then rendered in it.

What an uploaded template can carry

  • Structure: which layouts are allowed, chrome defaults, an outline and house-style rules. These are the same fields as a built-in template.
  • Fonts: woff2 files for headings and body, with a fallback. The uploader confirms they have the right to serve them, and the fonts are only sent to viewers of decks that use the template.
  • Logo: one version for light slides and one for dark slides, and whether dark slides show it.
  • Title art: an SVG or PNG behind the title and close slides, or a built-in generator with a colour (waves, grid, none).
  • Ornament: up to six dots above the title, such as brand colours.
  • Its own palette, checked against the same contrast bars as the built-ins. It can be locked so decks on this template stay on brand.
  • Custom layouts (Q9): HTML using only the template's tokens, with typed slots declared in a schema, the same way a built-in layout is defined. The server validates slides against the schema. The HTML is sanitised and rendered without scripts, and a person reviews each layout before the template is published.

What it can't carry

  • Free CSS or scripts. Styling outside a layout would break the house style and the contrast checks, and scripts are a security hole on a shared server. A custom layout's CSS is scoped to that layout and can only use tokens.
  • Animation. The drifting waves import as still art.

The house style the agent gets back

Returned by create_deck and list_templates. An uploaded template can add its own rules. SW-3 lint later checks the rules it can measure.

  1. One headline per slide, written as a sentence that makes the point. "Lead time fell as the slices got smaller", not "Lead time".
  2. A kicker above it names the topic in two to four words.
  3. One idea per slide: one layout's worth of content, and at most one callout.
  4. Speaker notes on every slide: what to say, and what to cut if short of time.
  5. Colour means something: positive for what comes back, negative for what to avoid, accent for the point. Never for decoration.
  6. Mark optional material as a deep dive instead of cutting it or squeezing it in.

Q1 · Which built-in palettes ship in SW-2?

A palette is now only colour values plus an automatic contrast test, so each one is cheap. Cobalt is required because it is SW-1's default.

Recommended: all four. Ember's serif headings and Plum's rounder cards went with the theme. If you want a serif look, that is a built-in template (note it below).

Answered 2026-11-16

Q2 · How does a deck change its colours?

The Measure counts decks on a built-in look "with no custom CSS". The more a deck can override, the less the palettes protect it.

Answered 2026-11-16

Q3 · What is a template?

With no themes, the template also holds type, shape, art and logo. This changes what create_deck returns, and what an upload can bring.

Not queued

Q4 · Can the template and palette change after the deck is made?

A palette only changes colours, so switching it is always safe. A template also changes which layouts are allowed, and may lock the palette.

Hand-built decks mark single slides by hand, and list them on each divider by hand.
